# Business Profile

Every claim the agent makes about the business must trace to this file.
If it is not written here, the agent does not say it.

This file is written by the `interview-business` skill (Phase 0) and
re-run whenever anything material about the business changes — a new
offer, a retired product, a new case study, a pricing change. Every
bracketed section below is a prompt for that interview, not filler text:
the interview replaces each bracket with the operator's own words, and
nothing downstream may add a fact this file doesn't contain.

## What we sell

[Interview: get the product or service list in the operator's own
words — what it is, what problem it solves, and what "done" looks like
for a customer. Capture packaging (one offer vs. a menu of offers) and
anything explicitly out of scope, so the agent knows what it must never
claim the business does.]

## Who we serve

[Interview: get the kinds of customers the business actually serves
today, in the operator's language — company types, buyer roles, and any
segments the business has learned it serves especially well or poorly.
This is the qualitative complement to `icp.md`'s firmographic filters:
`icp.md` says who scores well, this section says who the business is
built for and why.]

## Proof

[Interview: get named case studies, customer references, testimonials,
and concrete metrics (e.g., "cut onboarding time from 6 weeks to 9
days for Acme Co"), each with a name, a number, or both — never a vague
claim like "great results." If a metric can't be named or sourced by
the operator right now, it does not go in this section, and the agent
must never invent one to fill the gap. An empty subsection here is
correct until the operator supplies real proof.]

## Pricing

[Interview: get the price range, the pricing structure (flat fee,
per-seat, retainer, usage-based, custom quote), and what moves a
prospect from the low end of the range to the high end — deal size,
scope, urgency, contract length. This is what lets the agent recognize
a pricing question that falls outside the stated range and escalate it
instead of guessing.]

## Differentiators

[Interview: get what the operator says makes this business different
from the alternatives a prospect is actually comparing it to —
including the alternative of doing nothing. Prefer specific, defensible
claims over generic ones ("we ship in half the time because X" beats
"we're faster").]

## Disqualifiers

[Interview: get who the business declines to work with and why — bad
past fits, unworkable engagement types, industries or company shapes
the operator has learned not to take on. These feed the hard
disqualifiers in `icp.md`'s Anti-signals section; anything listed here
should also be checkable as an anti-signal so a bad-fit lead is caught
before it consumes research budget.]

## Voice

[Interview: get three to five words the operator would use to describe
how the business should sound in writing, plus two example sentences
in that voice — one that could open a cold email, one that could answer
a tough question on a call. These examples are what the Approacher and
Sales-call-specialist imitate; a voice description without examples is
too abstract to write from.]
